## Why

Issue #219: maxWorkers is one count for every briefed worker whatever its model, but the cap matters for the frontier tier (dear, and two already saturate a shared resource) while standard and economy workers are cheap and could run five at a time. Today a standard tool card queues behind two frontier cards that are themselves waiting on a resource. The spawn record and the brief header already carry the tier and tierMap maps tier to model, so a per-tier count needs no new state. The issue's suggestion that maxWorkers 0 mean "no total" is not taken: 0 keeps meaning the built-in 2 (the userConfig default is 0, so that would lift every repo's cap at once); a tier entry is a second, lower ceiling under the total, and a person who wants cheap tiers at five raises maxWorkers as well. Closes #219.

## Done when

It is parsed in hooks/lib/repoconfig.ts like spendByTier (a bad key or value is named and dropped, the rest kept), listed in REPO_KEYS, merged key by key across the layers (the maps line of docs/configuration.md gains it), present in .claude-plugin/plugin.json userConfig beside maxWorkers, in hooks/lib/configtable.ts (group Workers and models, summary at most 90 chars, a map render that prints `frontier 2, standard 5`), in hooks/lib/configcmd.ts (an `in effect:` note `none: maxWorkers alone` when empty), in hooks/lib/init.ts beside _maxWorkers with a one-line comment, and in hooks/lib/update.ts. The existing config tests that hold the option table complete both ways pass with the count raised by one.
- Pure scheduler, hooks/lib/scheduler.ts: a briefed spawn of tier T has a slot when the running plus starting workers stay under maxWorkers AND, when maxWorkersByTier has T, the running plus starting workers of tier T stay under that entry; a tier the map lacks takes the total alone. `hasSlot` and `readyCount` keep their signatures for callers that pass no tier and gain a form that takes the tier counts. `planQueue` takes each queued row's tier and the per-tier free counts beside the total free count: it walks the queue head first and marks a row ready when a total slot and a slot of its tier are both free, consuming both; a row whose tier is full is skipped without taking a slot, so a row behind it of another tier is ready in its place; order within a tier is unchanged; a row waiting on a resource still waits as before. The spawn hook and dispatch read a worker's tier from its spawn record (the field the record already carries) and a starting token's tier from a `startingTiers` map kept beside startingResources; a queued row's tier comes from its brief header (`tier=`) read where queuedResources reads `resources=`, standard when unreadable.

The debug line `queued …: N workers hold the M slots` says `the 2 frontier slots` when the tier cap is what held.
